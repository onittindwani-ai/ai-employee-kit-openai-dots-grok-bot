## OpenAI Dots

### 1. Introducing dots (OpenAI announcement, dated September 29, 2026, with an update on November 3, 2026)

https://openai.com/index/introducing-dots/ (Primary, read 2026-11-06)

Facts used:

- Dots are described as "always-on agents". They have their own cloud computer and can work towards your goals 24/7.
- "Through our ecosystem of plugins, they can readily connect to over 4,000 apps."
- You can reach your dot through ChatGPT (desktop, web and mobile), Slack and Teams, and by voice call. Texting was described as coming soon.
- Your dot can run a project "even while it's working on several others".
- You start with your primary dot. "Over time, we envision teams of dots working together on your behalf" (so teams of dots are a future plan, not a current feature).
- Proactive research in the background uses connected apps with tools restricted to be read only, which cannot send messages, change app content, or control your browser or computer.
- You choose which apps dots can access. Dots start with built in rules for when to act alone and when to ask. Custom Rules let you allow specific actions, require approval, or block them. Built in safety requirements always apply.
- Activity View lets you follow progress, including background work.
- OpenAI says it does not use content from ChatGPT Business, Enterprise or Edu workspaces to improve its models by default, and on personal plans you can control whether dots' conversations and work are used.
- "In the future, you'll be able to add more dots."
- Certain sensitive tasks, such as changing a password, always stay with you.
- "Dots can still make mistakes, so always review consequential work."
- Rollout began in ChatGPT for Pro and Business Premium users in eligible markets. Enterprise users can try the beta when their admin enables it.
- To get started: create your first dot in the ChatGPT desktop app or your desktop browser, connect your apps, and let it introduce itself.

Not used from this page: the model names and any plan or cost details. This kit states no prices.

### 2. Getting started with your dot (OpenAI Help Center)

https://help.openai.com/en/articles/20001530-getting-started-with-your-dot (Primary, read 2026-11-06, page said "Updated: yesterday")

Facts used:

- Your dot has its own cloud computer, works across the apps you choose to connect, and remembers context. It can keep making progress between conversations.
- You can create your dot in the ChatGPT mobile app (iOS or Android), the desktop app, or desktop web. Mobile web is not supported.
- Each member can create one personal dot (stated for Enterprise, Edu and Healthcare workspaces).
- You can ask your dot to set a reminder or run a recurring check, and manage scheduled tasks in its profile or in the Scheduled section.
- You can connect your personal email. At launch, a dot cannot have its own email address.
- Connected apps: on mobile, Customize > Plugins. Permission to read may not include permission to change or send.
- Custom rules: on mobile, Customize > Custom rules. The four behaviours are "Take action without asking", "Take action if pre-approved", "Ask before taking action" and "Hand off to you". "Pre-approved" means you explicitly asked for the action in your prompt.
- Asking for a draft does not authorize sending it.
- You can pause your dot from its profile.
- Your dot's cloud browser has its own sign in sessions. Some steps, such as changing a password, must be done by you.
- Plans: rolling out to Pro users aged 18 and older outside the European Economic Area, Switzerland and the UK; Business Premium in supported regions; generally available to eligible Enterprise, Edu and Healthcare workspaces.

### 3. Dots docs (OpenAI)

https://learn.chatgpt.com/docs/dots (Primary, read 2026-11-06; developers.openai.com/codex/dots redirects here)

Facts used:

- Your dot is described as an always on agent that keeps work moving across your tools and projects.
- You reach the same dot in ChatGPT, Slack, Teams, or a call. Its notes carry forward across conversations and contact methods.
- Your dot can run background agents on several things at once while you keep talking to it.
- Your dot can delegate work to Codex. This page describes creating the dot in ChatGPT, not in Codex.
- Stopping a task does not undo actions it has already completed.

## Grok Bot (xAI)

### 4. Introducing Grok Bot (xAI announcement, dated Aug 11, 2026)

https://x.ai/news/introducing-grok-bot (Primary, read 2026-11-06)

Facts used:

- "Grok Bot is your team of always-on agents." They have their own computer, work inside tools and apps, and keep working 24/7.
- "Bots share a computer of their own in the cloud, so jobs do not stall when you step away."
- They sign into the tools you already use and work across apps, tools and websites, "including platforms with no clean API or MCP".
- "Grok Bot gives you a small team that can work in parallel." Bots can message each other, share context in threads, and coordinate in a group chat.
- "They finish jobs end to end, and only come back when something needs your approval."
- Learning a workflow by watching: "The best way for a Bot to learn your workflow is to ask it to follow along the next time you do a job. It watches the steps and remembers how you like the work done." It "saves your workflow as a routine, takes your corrections, and runs it on its own next time".
- Grok Bot is in beta. It is available for SuperGrok, SuperGrok Plus and SuperGrok Heavy, and for Cursor Pro, Pro+, Ultra, and Cursor Teams Standard and Premium subscribers, on desktop and iOS. Enterprise users can join a waitlist.
- Grok Bot comes with its own usage, separate from Grok and Cursor plans.
- The page describes running several bots in parallel, with a chief of staff bot managing specialist bots.
- The page's own example jobs include sales outbound, demo readiness, pipeline operations (CRM hygiene and a Monday scoreboard), account follow up, and an operations bot that seats new hires and processes invoices from Gmail. (Used only as context for which jobs fit. This kit makes no performance claims from them.)

Not used: any price. The page lists no price for Grok Bot, so this kit states none.

### 5. Grok Bot 101 (xAI Grok Bot Guides, dated Sep 11, 2026)

https://x.ai/bot/guides/grok-bot-101 (Primary, read 2026-11-06, written by an xAI team member)

Facts used:

- A bot is a set of instructions. Its settings are three fields: Name, Title and Description, set in chat or in Settings.
- You can teach a workflow by recording a task or by writing a prompt.
- Grok Bot supports the same MCP servers, plugins and skills as Cursor. You can connect several accounts per service.
- Three ways to talk to a bot: chat, routines and triggers (a bot can set its own schedule or listen for events, such as a Slack thread or a GitHub PR), and other bots.
- Rules are written in plain language in Settings > General > Agent. A separate review agent checks proposed actions and can allow, block, or escalate to you. Allow and block lists steer that reviewer. "You are trusting the model to follow what you wrote."
- "If you log into a site with one bot, every other bot can reach that site too."
- If you log into a shopping site on a computer the agent can use, it can technically buy things, the same way a human could. Bots are kept in check by permissions, a reviewer, and allow and block lists.
- The author describes a specialist bot asking a Chief of Staff bot for a judgment call.
- When a flow needs you (for example a CAPTCHA or a 2FA login), the bot can hand the desktop back. It can also send you a secure form for a password or an API key.
